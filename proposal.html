02How a new handle wood design is created and published

Brief §3 asks whether to work from photographs of a finished handle or from a modular system. We provide both and say which to use when.

Workflow A · No-Code 8-Zone Modular Builder

Recommended for everyday use. Each Fusion 360 handle shape is prepared with eight independent material channels in six construction groups:

  • Zone 1a / 1b left and right body wings
  • Zone 2a / 2b outer and centre cheek stripes (1–8 stripe layout)
  • Zone 3 centre core / Padauk gaps
  • Zone 4 single side flank stripe
  • Zone 5 butt end-cap wood
  • Zone 6 CAD-following rim inlay

The live demo already synthesizes high-resolution, photo-grain textures and applies them directly to the CAD handle mesh. In the delivered WordPress Admin, Resonant picks a wood species per zone from a drop-down and saves. The same zone set is applied automatically to flared, straight, anatomical and penhold shapes.

Resonant doesUploads front and back flat photos of a new species once (reusable across all models); combines zones; publishes.
We doNothing for combinations of the supplied species, after Milestone 2.
Time< 2 min per design
Cost€0

Limits. Stripes run straight along the handle and meet at straight borders. Diagonal splices, curved inlays or asymmetrical marquetry cannot be described by these straight longitudinal zones.

Workflow B · Photo-to-CAD UV Projection

For complex or irregular designs. Resonant sends 2 to 3 photographs of the finished handle (top, back, butt) together with the existing Fusion 360 shape. We register the photographs to the handle UV layout with our perspective-unwrap script, cut them to the handle boundary, and build the texture set.

Resonant doesPhotographs the handle flat on a neutral background, sharp, no strong shadows, a ruler in frame.
We doUnwrap, register, crop to the handle zone, check end grain and edges in 3D, hand the finished asset over; Resonant publishes it from the same admin screen.
Time~30 min per design
Cost€12 / design · €100 for a batch of 10 across all 4 handle shapes

Limits. The photograph shows one specimen; the pattern is reproduced as photographed, not as a rule. Strong perspective, reflections or a handle photographed on a curved background reduce accuracy and may need a re-shoot.

Answer to “is it realistic to work from finished-handle photographs?” Yes, as Approach B, and the live demo shows it: Handle 2 (padauk, three hornbeam pinstripes, white oak butt cap) is registered from your Handle 2.jpeg inside the 102.00 mm flared handle zone and does not run onto the blade face. Use 07 Compare with Your Photo (Same Angle in 3D) in the demo to check the boundary.

03WooCommerce plugin architecture

The 3D viewer runs in the browser. Everything that decides money or manufacturing is decided on the server.

Server-side checks, in order

  1. Every selected component exists and is enabled.
  2. Compatibility rules from the admin pass; otherwise the request is rejected.
  3. Price = model base price + sum of component prices, computed on the server.
  4. Weight = model weight + sum of component weights; shown with ±5 g.
  5. Configuration is stored with a short code and written to the cart item, order meta and e-mails.

Admin screens (no code)

ScreenFields
Blade modelsName, size S / M / L / DEF, GLB, base price, weight, layer list (material name, thickness optional)
Wood speciesFront and back flat photos, normal / roughness maps (optional), display name
Handle designsZone-to-species assignment, or a prepared texture set (Approach B), price, weight
Handle shapes and internal constructionsGLB, price, weight
Labels, flags, engravingImage files, price, weight; engraving price and character limit
